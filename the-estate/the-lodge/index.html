The morning

Two suites, and nobody drives to a salon.

The Willow Room is a private hair and makeup studio inside the building everyone slept in: three stations, mirrors and lights along the wall, and a sofa for whoever is next. The morning of a wedding is the part that runs late when it is spread across a town, and here it is not spread at all — the party wakes up, eats, and sits down in the next room.

The Mahogany Suite is the same building at the other end of the day: a pool table, a card table and a seventy-inch screen, for the people who are not finished at midnight and should not have to be quiet.

  • The Willow RoomA private hair and makeup studio, for the morning of.
  • The Mahogany SuiteA pool table, a card table and a seventy-inch screen.
The kitchen

Built for a crowd, not for a couple.

Two refrigerators, a double oven and an island long enough to work around. It is the reason the Lodge holds the rehearsal night, the breakfast, and the hour on the wedding morning when fourteen people want coffee at once. Nothing has to be catered to be eaten here.

The beds

Fourteen, under one roof.

A primary suite with a king bed and its own bathroom, three more bedrooms, and four full bathrooms — so the wedding party or the largest family group stays together instead of being split between cottages and a hotel in town. Outside there is a wraparound porch, a fire pit, and the valley in front of it. Guests of the Lodge have the pool to themselves.

Between Saturdays the Lodge is open as a stay on its own terms, and it pairs with the cottages when the group is larger than fourteen.
