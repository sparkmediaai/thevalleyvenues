The Estate · Lookout Deck · Where the light goes

Where the mountain turns gold.

A railed deck out over the valley, facing Lookout Mountain. At six the light goes across it tip to tip, and everyone stops talking.

What it is

The view no other estate in the region has.

The deck is built out over the fall of the land, so the valley drops away beneath the rail and the mountain fills everything beyond it. It is where cocktails are poured after the ceremony — or where the ceremony itself is held, at the rail, with the mountain for a backdrop — where the first look tends to happen in the morning, and where the wedding party ends up whenever nobody has told them where to be.

Guests walk here from the meadow. It is a thousand feet, the longest walk of the whole weekend, and it goes uphill toward the light — which is the point. Nobody is shuttled, nobody is released in groups, and nobody is looking for their keys.

  • HoldsA ceremony at the rail, facing the mountain. Cocktail hour. The first look. Small dinners. Anything that wants the view.
  • FacesLookout Mountain and the evening light, across the whole width of the valley.
  • The hourGold at six, tip to tip, for about half an hour.
  • SeatsUp to a hundred and fifty for a ceremony, on fifteen thousand square feet of deck.
  • SitsA thousand feet from the meadow. Six hundred and fifty from Davis Hall.
  • ThenDown to the hall for dinner, on foot, in the last of it.
